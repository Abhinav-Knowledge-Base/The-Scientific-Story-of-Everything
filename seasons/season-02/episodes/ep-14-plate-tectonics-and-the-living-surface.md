## Introduction: The Restless Skin

Look at an ordinary world map.

Run your eyes down the Atlantic Ocean. Trace the bulging eastern coast of South America, and look across the water at the concave indentation of western Africa. 

The two coastlines fit together like pieces of a torn jigsaw puzzle.

```
                           THE JIGSAW OF CONTINENTS
                           
               SOUTH AMERICA                         AFRICA
                    .-.                                .-.
                   /   \                              /   \
                  |     \       ATLANTIC             /     |
                  |      \       OCEAN              /      |
                   \      '--------.       .-------'      /
                    \               \     /              /
                     '---.           \   /           .--'
                          \           \ /           /
                           '--------'  v  '--------'
                           
       IDENTICAL FOSSIL BEDS & GEOLOGICAL STRATA ON OPPOSITE SIDES:
       - Mesosaurus (Freshwater reptile unable to swim across saltwater oceans!)
       - Glossopteris (Fern flora spanning South America, Africa, India, Antarctica!)
       - Matching Paleozoic glacial striations in tropical Brazil and the Congo!
```

In 1912, German meteorologist and polar explorer **Alfred Wegener** presented a revolutionary hypothesis: the continents were not fixed in place. 

Hundreds of millions of years ago, all the landmasses of Earth had been united in a single, colossal supercontinent: **Pangaea** (Greek for "All-Earth"). 

Over deep geological time, Pangaea had fractured, and the fragmented continents had drifted thousands of kilometers across the globe.

For half a century, the geological establishment ridiculed Wegener’s theory. Eminent geophysicists pointed out an insurmountable flaw: Wegener could propose no viable physical mechanism. 

How could solid granite continents plow through the rigid, crystalline basalt floor of the ocean like icebreakers through sea ice?

It took until the 1960s—armed with sonar maps of the deep ocean floor, sensitive magnetometers, and global seismic networks—for science to realize that Wegener had been right about the motion, but wrong about the mechanism.

Continents do not plow through the ocean floor. 

The continents and the ocean floors are carried together on giant, rigid rafts of stone: **Tectonic Plates**. 

Earth’s brittle surface is cracked into a dynamic mosaic of wandering plates that slide, collide, grind, and plunge back into the mantle, continually remaking the face of our planet.

Plate tectonics is the master organizing framework of modern geology. 

It is the engine that builds mountain ranges, opens oceans, sparks earthquakes, fuels volcanic chains, and regulates the global carbon thermostat that has kept Earth habitable for billions of years.

## 1. The Seafloor Spreading Revolution: The Magnetic Tape Recorder

The breakthrough that transformed continental drift into modern plate tectonics occurred in the dark, uncharted depths of the world's oceans.

During World War II and the Cold War, navies mapped the ocean floor to navigate submarines. 

At Columbia University’s Lamont Geological Observatory, cartographer **Marie Tharp** and geologist **Bruce Heezen** synthesized thousands of sonar depth soundings. 

In 1957, Tharp made an astonishing discovery: running down the center of the Atlantic Ocean was not a featureless muddy plain, but an immense, continuous mountain range—the **Mid-Atlantic Ridge**—crowned by a deep central rift valley where the crust was visibly pulling apart. 

Tharp had mapped the largest geological structure on Earth: a **$65,000\text{-kilometer}$ global mid-ocean ridge system** wrapping around the planet like the seams on a baseball.

```
                         THE SEAFLOOR SPREADING CONVEYOR BELT
                         
                                  MID-OCEAN RIDGE
                                  (Rift Valley)
                                        |
                 <=== Spreading         v        Spreading ===>
                      Direction                  Direction
        ================================+================================
        [ OLDER BASALT ] [ YOUNG BASALT ] [ YOUNG BASALT ] [ OLDER BASALT ]
        --------------------------------+--------------------------------
                                  ^   ^   ^
                                 /    |    \
                                /  MAGMA   \
                               /  UPWELLING \
                              /              \
        =================================================================
                               SOLID ASTHENOSPHERE
                            (Decompression Melting!)
```

### Harry Hess and Seafloor Spreading (1962)
In 1962, Princeton geologist and rear admiral **Harry Hess** connected Tharp’s rift valley to mantle convection. 

The ocean floor, Hess proposed, was a gigantic conveyor belt:
1. Hot, buoyant peridotite from the mantle rises beneath mid-ocean ridges.
2. As it ascends, pressure drops, triggering **decompression melting** without adding heat.
3. Basaltic magma erupts into the central rift, freezing against seawater to create **fresh oceanic crust**.
4. The newly formed crust spreads laterally away from the ridge axis at rates of **$1$ to $16\text{ centimeters per year}$**.
5. Millions of years later, as the oceanic crust cools and grows denser, it plunges back into the mantle at deep-sea trenches.

Continents do not plow through the ocean floor; **they are passive passengers riding on the conveyor belt**.

### The Paleomagnetic Proof: The Vine-Matthews-Morley Hypothesis
The smoking-gun proof of seafloor spreading arrived in 1963 from British geophysicists **Fred Vine** and **Drummond Matthews**, and independently Canadian geophysicist **Lawrence Morley**.

As basaltic lava erupts at a mid-ocean ridge and cools below the **Curie Temperature** of magnetite ($580^\circ\text{C}$), tiny magnetic iron crystals align themselves like microscopic compass needles with Earth’s magnetic field. 

Once the rock solidifies, that magnetic orientation is frozen into the stone permanently.

Because Earth’s geodynamo spontaneously reverses its magnetic polarity every few hundred thousand years ([Chapter 13](ep-13-inside-the-earth.md)), the seafloor acts as an immense **magnetic tape recorder**:

```
                   PALEOMAGNETIC ZEBRA STRIPES ON THE SEAFLOOR
                   
      WEST OF RIDGE                    RIDGE AXIS                    EAST OF RIDGE
      
      [ N ]  [ R ]  [ N ]  [ R ]  [ N ]   | v |   [ N ]  [ R ]  [ N ]  [ R ]  [ N ]
      ====================================+===+====================================
      <--- Spreading Direction                    Spreading Direction --->
      
      PERFECT MIRROR-IMAGE SYMMETRY ACROSS THE RIDGE AXIS!
      [N] = Normal Polarity (Field points North, like today)
      [R] = Reversed Polarity (Field points South)
```

Towing magnetometers behind research ships across the seafloor, oceanographers discovered alternating bands of normal (positive anomaly) and reversed (negative anomaly) magnetism running parallel to the ridge axis.

The pattern was **a perfect, symmetrical mirror image on both sides of the ridge**.

Furthermore, radiometric dating of drill cores confirmed that:
* Basalt directly at the ridge crest has an age of **zero years**.
* The age of the basalt increases linearly with distance away from the ridge in both directions.
* Nowhere in the world does oceanic crust exceed **$200\text{ million years}$ in age** (less than $5\%$ of Earth's total lifespan). 

All older oceanic crust has been systematically returned to the mantle.

## 2. The Three Boundaries: The Geometry of a Fractured Shell

Earth’s outer shell is divided into approximately **a dozen major tectonic plates** (such as the Pacific, North American, Eurasian, African, and Antarctic plates) and dozens of smaller microplates.

The plates consist of the **Lithosphere**—the brittle crust plus the uppermost rigid mantle—which glides over the warm, ductile **Asthenosphere** ($100\text{ to } 410\text{ km}$ depth).

The interactions between plates occur along three distinct types of geological boundaries:

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                               THE THREE TECTONIC PLATE BOUNDARIES                                  |
+--------------------------+-----------------------+-----------------------+-------------------------+
| BOUNDARY TYPE            | RELATIVE MOTION       | GEOLOGICAL PROCESSES  | PROMINENT REAL-WORLD    |
|                          |                       | & FEATURES            | EXAMPLES                |
+--------------------------+-----------------------+-----------------------+-------------------------+
| DIVERGENT                | Plates pull apart     | Seafloor spreading;   | Mid-Atlantic Ridge;     |
| (Constructive)           | (<---  --->)          | decompression basalt  | East Pacific Rise;      |
|                          |                       | volcanism; rift zones.| East African Rift.      |
+--------------------------+-----------------------+-----------------------+-------------------------+
| CONVERGENT               | Plates collide        | Subduction; deep-sea  | Mariana Trench;         |
| (Destructive)            | (--->  <---)          | trenches; volcanic    | Andes Mountains;        |
|                          |                       | arcs; mountain belts. | Himalayas.              |
+--------------------------+-----------------------+-----------------------+-------------------------+
| TRANSFORM                | Plates grind past     | Strike-slip shearing; | San Andreas Fault (CA); |
| (Conservative)           | ( ^ v )               | shallow earthquakes;  | North Anatolian Fault   |
|                          |                       | zero crust created.   | (Turkey).               |
+--------------------------+-----------------------+-----------------------+-------------------------+
```

### 1. Divergent Boundaries: The Birth of Oceans
Where plates pull apart, upwelling mantle rock melts by decompression, generating basaltic volcanism. 

Divergence begins inside continents as a continental rift (such as the **East African Rift System**). 

As the crust stretches and thins, normal faults drop blocks of land into deep rift valleys hosting volcanic chains and lakes (Lake Tanganyika, Lake Malawi).

Eventually, the continental crust tears completely apart. Seawater rushes in, creating a narrow young sea (such as the **Red Sea**). 

Continued spreading pushes the landmasses apart into a full-scale ocean basin like the Atlantic.

### 2. Convergent Boundaries: The Great Destructive Engines
Where two plates collide, the denser plate is forced downward into the hot mantle in a process called **Subduction**.

Convergent boundaries occur in three distinct flavors:

```
                         THE THREE CONVERGENT FLAVORS
                         
   A. OCEAN-CONTINENT SUBDUCTION                 B. OCEAN-OCEAN SUBDUCTION
   Dense oceanic plate subducts;                Older, colder oceanic plate subducts;
   Hydration melting creates ANDESITE ARCS!     Volcanic ISLAND ARCS (Japan, Marianas)!
   
          Trench          Volcanoes                    Trench          Island Arc
            v                v                           v                v
     ~~~~~~~~\              / \                   ~~~~~~~\              / \
              \            /   \                          \            /   \
     [OCEAN]   \____[CONTINENTAL CRUST]          [OCEAN]   \____[VOLCANIC ISLAND]
                \                                           \
                 v                                           v
                 
   C. CONTINENT-CONTINENT COLLISION (The Mountain Factory)
   Two buoyant continental blocks smash together; NEITHER CAN SUBDUCT!
   Crust doubles in thickness; mountains thrust skyward: THE HIMALAYAS!
```

* **Oceanic-Continental (e.g., The Andes, Cascades):** Dense oceanic lithosphere plunges beneath buoyant continental crust, carving a deep ocean trench. 
  
  As the subducting slab descends, heating drives water out of hydrated ocean-floor minerals (serpentine, clays). This released water ascends into the overlying mantle wedge, acting as a chemical flux that **lowers the melting temperature of mantle peridotite by hundreds of degrees** (**Flux Melting**). 
  
  The resulting magma ascends through the continental crust, differentiating into silica-rich andesite and dacite, erupting as explosive stratovolcanoes and uplifting continental mountain ranges.

* **Oceanic-Oceanic (e.g., Japan, The Mariana Arc, The Aleutians):** The older, colder, denser of two oceanic plates subducts, forming a deep ocean trench (the Mariana Trench plunges to $10,994\text{ meters}$) and curved chains of volcanic islands (**Island Arcs**).

* **Continental-Continental (e.g., The Himalayas):** When an entire ocean basin has been subducted, the two trailing continents collide (such as the Indian plate colliding with the Eurasian plate beginning $\sim 50\text{ million years ago}$). 

  Because continental crust is buoyant ($\rho \approx 2.7\text{ g/cm}^3$) and thick, **it refuses to sink into the dense mantle ($\rho \approx 3.3\text{ g/cm}^3$)**. 

  Subduction jams. 

  The continents crumple, fold, and thrust over one another, doubling the thickness of the crust to over **$70\text{ kilometers}$** and thrusting the peaks of the Himalayas and the Tibetan Plateau skyward.

### 3. Transform Boundaries: Shearing Earthquakes
Where plates slide horizontally past one another along strike-slip faults, lithosphere is neither created nor destroyed. 

Friction locks the fault planes together while the tectonic plates continue moving at centimeters per year. Elastic strain energy accumulates in the deformed rocks until frictional resistance is overcome, rupturing violently in major shallow earthquakes (such as the **San Andreas Fault** in California).

## 3. The Driving Engine: What Actually Moves the Plates?

For decades, introductory geology textbooks depicted tectonic plates as passive rafts floating on giant, circular convection cells in the mantle, driven by friction along the plate base (**Basal Drag**).

Modern mantle dynamics and numerical modeling have revealed that this picture is largely inverted: **The plates are not dragged by the mantle; the plates drive their own motion**.

```
                           THE TECTONIC FORCE BALANCE
                           
      RIDGE PUSH (~ 10%)                                    SLAB PULL (~ 90% OF TOTAL FORCE!)
      Gravity pushes elevated                               Cold, dense oceanic slab plunges
      ridge down slope into basin                           into asthenosphere; PULLS THE
                                                            ENTIRE PLATE BEHIND IT!
            ^                                                                 |
           / \                                                                v
          /   \                                                               |
         /     \______________________________________________________________|
        [  MID-OCEAN RIDGE  ]          OCEANIC LITHOSPHERE                    |
        (Elevated 2-3 km high)         (Grows thicker & denser as it ages)    |
                                                                              v
                                                                        [ METAMORPHISM ]
                                                                        Basalt -> Eclogite
                                                                        (Density jumps to 3.5 g/cm³!)
```

The motion of tectonic plates is governed by three primary forces:

### 1. Slab Pull (The Dominant Engine: $\approx 90\%$ of Driving Force)
As fresh oceanic lithosphere travels away from a mid-ocean ridge, conductive cooling to ocean water causes the lithosphere to cool and thicken from $5\text{ km}$ to over $100\text{ km}$ over tens of millions of years. 

Because rock shrinks as it cools, its density increases:
$$\rho_{\text{lithosphere}} > \rho_{\text{asthenosphere}}$$

By the time oceanic crust reaches an age of $20\text{ to } 30\text{ million years}$, **it becomes denser than the warm asthenospheric mantle beneath it**. 

The plate is gravitationally unstable. 

Once subduction initiates, the heavy, cold slab sinks under its own weight into the mantle like a lead anchor dropped off a boat. 

Furthermore, as the basaltic oceanic crust descends past $40\text{ to } 60\text{ kilometers}$ depth, pressure forces its minerals to undergo a metamorphic phase transition into **Eclogite**—an ultra-dense rock composed of garnet and omphacite pyroxene ($\rho \approx 3.5\text{ g/cm}^3$). 

This density jump acts as a turbocharger, pulling the trailing plate behind it with enormous gravitational force.

Plates attached to subducting slabs (such as the Pacific Plate, moving at $8\text{ to } 10\text{ cm/year}$) move significantly faster than plates with no major subducting edges (such as the North American and Eurasian plates, moving at $1\text{ to } 3\text{ cm/year}$).

### 2. Ridge Push ($\approx 10\%$ of Driving Force)
Mid-ocean ridges stand **two to three kilometers higher** than the surrounding abyssal plains because newly formed lithosphere is hot, expanded, and buoyant. 

Gravity pulls the elevated ridge mass down the slope toward the abyssal plains, exerting an outward lateral shove.

### 3. Slab Suction and Mantle Drag
As a subducting slab plunges into the mantle, it induces viscous hydrodynamic circulation in the surrounding asthenosphere, sucking neighboring plates toward the trench.

## 4. The Wilson Cycle: The Breathing of Continents

Continents do not wander randomly across the face of the Earth. 

In 1966, pioneering Canadian geophysicist **J. Tuzo Wilson** discovered that the opening and closing of ocean basins follows a rhythmic, cyclic pattern spanning **$400\text{ to } 600\text{ million years}$**: **The Wilson Cycle**.

## 5. Plate Tectonics as the Biological Engine

Why is plate tectonics essential for life?

If Earth were a "stagnant-lid" planet like Mars or Venus—possessing a single, unbroken planetary crust—complex life could never have evolved. 

Plate tectonics is the master circulatory system of the biosphere:

```
                        TECTONIC COUPLING TO THE BIOSPHERE
                        
       1. NUTRIENT REPLENISHMENT            2. VOLCANIC CO2 DEGASSING        3. HABITAT DIVERSITY
       Continental collisions uplift        Subduction volcanoes replenish   Continental rifting
       mountains; erosion washes            atmospheric CO2, preventing      creates island continents,
       Phosphorus & Iron into seas!         Snowball Earth freeze-out!       spurring speciation!
                     \                               |                               /
                      \                              |                              /
                       v                             v                             v
       +-------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
       |                         A VIBRANT, EVOLVING BIOSPHERE                         |
       +-------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 1. The Nutrient Delivery Pump
Living organisms require vast quantities of bio-essential elements, most notably **Phosphorus (P)**—the indispensable structural backbone of DNA, RNA, and ATP energy storage.

Phosphorus does not exist in the atmosphere as a gas. Its sole reservoir on Earth is in rocks, specifically the mineral **Apatite** ($\text{Ca}_5(\text{PO}_4)_3(\text{F,Cl,OH})$).

If plate tectonics did not exist, rainfall and weathering would wash all surface phosphorus into the ocean depths within tens of millions of years, locking it permanently away in seafloor sediments. 

The oceans would become biological deserts.

Plate tectonics saves the biosphere by **Orogeny (Mountain Building)**:
* Colliding continents thrust ancient seafloor sediments back up to the tops of mountain ranges (which is why fossil marine shells are found on the summit of Mount Everest).
* Glaciers and rivers grind these mountains down, flushing fresh phosphorus, iron, and calcium back into the oceans, fertilizing phytoplankton blooms and powering the global food web.

### 2. Speciation Through Continental Drift
When a supercontinent fractures during the Wilson Cycle, landmasses drift apart across oceans. 

Populations of plants and animals become geographically isolated (**Allopatric Speciation**). 

Furthermore, continental breakup dramatically expands the area of shallow, sunlit **continental shelf seas**—the richest biological habitats in the marine realm. 

The breakup of Rodinia at $750\text{ Ma}$ set the stage for the **Cambrian Explosion of animal life** ([Chapter 18](../../season-01/episodes/ep-18-the-cambrian-explosion.md)), while the fragmentation of Pangaea drove the explosive adaptive radiation of dinosaurs and mammals ([Chapters 20–22](../../season-01/episodes/ep-20-the-age-of-reptiles.md)).

## 6. The Archean Frontier: When Did Plate Tectonics Begin?

While plate tectonics is undisputed today, one of the most contentious debates in modern geosciences concerns its origin: **When did modern-style subduction plate tectonics actually begin?**

Proposals range from the Hadean ($> 4.0\text{ Ga}$) to the Neoproterozoic ($< 1.0\text{ Ga}$):

```
                       THE COMPETING TECTONIC ONSET HYPOTHESES
                       
     HADEAN ONSET (> 4.0 Ga)            ARCHEAN ONSET (3.2 - 2.5 Ga)       NEOPROTEROZOIC ONSET (< 1.0 Ga)
     
     Jack Hills zircons show low-       Emergence of paired metamorphic    Appearance of blueschists and
     temp granitic melts requiring      belts; diamond inclusion shifts;   ophiolites; true modern cold,
     water-rich subduction zones!       first stable continental cratons.  steep subduction ignites!
```

### The Heat Dilemma of Early Earth
Three billion years ago, radioactive decay and primordial heat made Earth’s mantle **$200^\circ\text{C}$ to $300^\circ\text{C}$ hotter** than it is today. 

In a hotter mantle:
* Decompression melting at mid-ocean ridges was far more extensive, producing an oceanic crust that was **$20\text{ to } 30\text{ kilometers}$ thick** (compared to $7\text{ km}$ today).
* Thick oceanic crust is highly buoyant. It resists subduction, making modern-style steep slab pull physically difficult or impossible.

Many geophysicists argue that the early Earth operated in a **"Stagnant-Lid"** or **"Squishy-Lid"** regime:
* Heat was released through massive volcanic pipes (heat-pipe tectonics).
* Instead of lateral subduction, heavy, dense volcanic crust sank vertically down into the mantle in downward drips called **Sagduction**.

The transition to modern, lateral subduction plate tectonics was likely an episodic, gradual evolution occurring between **$3.0\text{ and } 2.0\text{ billion years ago}$**, permanently stabilizing Earth’s climate and surface environment.

## 8. The Honesty Beat: What We Know, What We Don't, and What We Need

### What We Confidently Know
1. **The continents are moving right now:** GPS satellite networks measure continental drift in real time, confirming that North America and Europe are separating at $2.5\text{ cm/year}$.
2. **Oceanic crust is completely recycled:** The oldest seafloor on Earth is less than $200\text{ million years}$ old, proving that oceanic plates are constantly born at ridges and destroyed at trenches.
3. **Slab pull drives the plates:** The dense metamorphic conversion of basalt into eclogite pulls oceanic plates down into the asthenosphere under their own gravitational weight.
4. **Plate tectonics maintains the biosphere:** Without tectonic nutrient recycling and the carbonate-silicate thermostat, the Earth would have frozen or lost its biosphere billions of years ago.

### What We Do Not Know
1. **When Subduction Truly Began:** Did modern plate tectonics begin as a global system $3.0\text{ Ga}$, or did it develop episodically from local proto-subduction zones?
2. **How Subduction Initiates:** What initial mechanical force breaks a strong, intact oceanic plate to start a brand new subduction zone?
3. **The Fate of Subducted Slabs in the Lower Mantle:** Do subducted slabs plunge all the way to the core-mantle boundary ($D''$ layer), or do they temporarily stall in the mantle transition zone?

### What Would Count as Better Evidence
* **Discovery of Hadean Ophiolites:** Finding an unambiguous sliver of ancient oceanic crust and sheeted dikes older than $3.8\text{ Ga}$ in cratonic greenstone belts.
* **Seismic Imaging of Subduction Initiation:** Capturing a modern proto-subduction zone in the act of nucleating (such as the Puysegur Trench south of New Zealand).
* **Supercomputer Rheological Mantle Models:** Next-generation 3D spherical mantle simulations resolving grain-scale damage mechanics and water weakening along fault interfaces.

## 9. Common Misconceptions to Refute

### Myth 1: "The continents float on a boiling ocean of liquid molten lava beneath the crust."
**Scientific Rebuttal:** The asthenosphere beneath the tectonic plates is **solid rock**. It is not molten magma. It behaves as a ductile solid that deforms through solid-state plastic creep over millions of years. Partial melting in the asthenosphere is typically less than $1\%$ to $2\%$ of rock volume.

### Myth 2: "Continental drift and plate tectonics are two completely different theories."
**Scientific Rebuttal:** Plate tectonics is the modern, mechanically verified evolution of continental drift. Alfred Wegener correctly recognized that the continents moved, but incorrectly believed they plowed through the ocean floor. Plate tectonics proved that both the continents and ocean basins are part of larger, unified rigid plates moving together over the mantle.

### Myth 3: "Earthquakes and volcanoes can occur anywhere on Earth with equal probability."
**Scientific Rebuttal:** Over $95\%$ of all earthquakes and volcanic eruptions on Earth occur along narrow, well-defined plate boundaries: the "Ring of Fire" surrounding the Pacific Ocean, mid-ocean rift systems, and transform faults. Intraplate earthquakes and volcanoes (such as Hawaii or Yellowstone) are rare exceptions caused by mantle plumes or ancient re-activated continental faults.

### Myth 4: "When continents collide, massive earthquakes open giant chasms that swallow cities."
**Scientific Rebuttal:** Earthquakes cause fault displacement, ground shaking, and localized surface ruptures, but faults do not open into bottomless chasms that swallow buildings. At convergent boundaries, plates are being compressed together under billions of tons of lithostatic pressure; there is no space to open subterranean voids.

## 10. Summary & Technical Glossary

* **Plate Tectonics:** The foundational geological theory establishing that Earth’s outer shell is divided into rigid lithospheric plates moving over a ductile asthenosphere.
* **Lithosphere:** The rigid, brittle outer layer of Earth ($0–100\text{ km}$) comprising the crust and the uppermost solid mantle.
* **Asthenosphere:** The warm, ductile mechanical layer of the upper mantle ($100–410\text{ km}$) capable of plastic solid-state flow under stress.
* **Seafloor Spreading:** The process where new oceanic crust is created by volcanic upwelling at mid-ocean ridges and spreads laterally away from the ridge axis.
* **Subduction Zone:** A convergent plate boundary where an oceanic plate plunges into the mantle beneath an overriding plate, marked by trenches and volcanic arcs.
* **Slab Pull:** The primary gravitational force driving plate tectonics, caused by cold, dense subducting oceanic lithosphere sinking into the lighter asthenosphere.
* **Wilson Cycle:** The cyclic opening and closing of ocean basins and assembly/breakup of supercontinents over timescales of $400$ to $600\text{ million years}$.
* **Eclogite:** An ultra-dense metamorphic rock formed from basalt at high pressures during subduction, accelerating slab pull.
* **Pangaea:** The most recent global supercontinent, assembled $\approx 300\text{ million years ago}$ and fractured $\approx 175\text{ million years ago}$.
* **Flux Melting:** The process where water released from a subducting slab lowers the melting point of the overlying mantle peridotite, generating arc volcanism.
